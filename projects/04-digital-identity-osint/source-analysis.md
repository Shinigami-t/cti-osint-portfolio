# Source Analysis — OSINT-004

## Purpose

Assess the reliability of the simulated sources used to investigate the relationship between Accounts A, B, and C.

All sources and observations in this exercise are fictional.

## Source Assessments

### SRC-01 — Account A Profile

**Source type:** Simulated public profile  
**Information collected:** Handle `NightSignal_7` and a profile link to Account C.

**Strengths:**
- The handle and link are directly observable in the scenario.
- The profile provides a starting point for further correlation.

**Limitations:**
- A profile can contain misleading or copied information.
- A profile link does not establish who controls either account.

**Assessment:** High confidence in the recorded observation; limited confidence in any claim about account ownership.

### SRC-02 — Account B Profile

**Source type:** Simulated public profile  
**Information collected:** Handle `NightSignal7`.

**Strengths:**
- The handle can be compared directly with Account A's handle.

**Limitations:**
- Similar usernames may result from coincidence, impersonation, or deliberate copying.
- No independent ownership verification is available.

**Assessment:** High confidence in the recorded handle; weak standalone evidence of common control.

### SRC-03 — Simulated Public Posts

**Source type:** Fictional public posts  
**Information collected:** A shared distinctive phrase and overlapping technical topics.

**Strengths:**
- Text and publication timing can be compared across accounts.
- Multiple observations may help develop a correlation hypothesis.

**Limitations:**
- Posts can be copied or influenced by the same public trend.
- Similar content may not be independent evidence.
- The scenario does not provide independently verified account-owner information.

**Assessment:** High confidence that the scenario records these similarities; moderate or lower evidential value for common ownership.

## Source Reliability vs. Information Credibility

Source reliability concerns how dependable a source generally is. Information credibility concerns whether a particular claim is accurate.

A source may reliably display a username without proving that the named persona owns the account.

## Analytical Safeguards

1. Separate recorded facts from analytical judgments.
2. Avoid treating multiple copies of the same claim as independent corroboration.
3. Consider impersonation, coincidence, and shared influences.
4. State the limits of the available evidence.
5. Do not infer a real person's identity from this fictional exercise.

## Conclusion

The simulated sources are adequate for practising account correlation, but they do not independently establish common ownership. Further independent evidence would be required for a stronger assessment.
