# Methodology — OSINT-004

## 1. Objective

Assess whether three fictional online accounts can reasonably be linked to the same online persona using simulated public information.

## 2. Investigation Process

### Step 1: Define the scope
Use fictional accounts and simulated observations only. Do not investigate real individuals or attempt to obtain private information.

### Step 2: Record the evidence
Assign each observation a unique evidence ID and document its source, reliability, limitations, and analytical relevance.

### Step 3: Evaluate sources
Distinguish between what a source directly shows and what an analyst infers from it. A reliable record of a username does not prove who owns the account.

### Step 4: Build a timeline
Arrange the recorded observations chronologically. Treat timestamps as unverified scenario data.

### Step 5: Correlate indicators
Compare usernames, profile links, repeated phrases, topics, and timing. Consider whether the indicators are independent or could share a common explanation.

### Step 6: Test alternative hypotheses
Consider coincidence, impersonation, copied content, and shared community interests before concluding that accounts have a common operator.

### Step 7: Assess confidence
Match the strength of the conclusion to the available evidence. State uncertainty and identify intelligence gaps.

## 3. Analytical Safeguards

- Separate observations from assumptions.
- Avoid treating similar usernames as proof of common ownership.
- Avoid double-counting related indicators.
- Consider evidence that could contradict the initial hypothesis.
- Do not attribute accounts to a real person without sufficient, independently corroborated evidence.
- Document limitations transparently.

## 4. Confidence Assessment

Confidence refers to the strength of the analytical judgment, not the reliability of a single observation.

The simulated observations support a possible relationship between the accounts, but they do not independently establish common control. The assessment must therefore remain qualified.

## 5. Limitations

This is a fictional training exercise. No real accounts were investigated, no external sources were collected, and no account ownership was verified.
