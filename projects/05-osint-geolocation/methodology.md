# Methodology — Project 05: Image Geolocation

## Objective
Determine the likely location depicted in a photograph using observable visual clues, metadata analysis, and independent corroboration.

## 1. Preserve the Target
- Retain the downloaded target image.
- Record its filename and image properties.
- Do not modify the original evidence file during analysis.

## 2. Inspect Metadata
- Use ExifTool to inspect available EXIF fields.
- Check for GPS coordinates, camera details, timestamps, and editing software.
- Treat metadata as supporting evidence, not definitive proof of location.

## 3. Record Visual Observations
- Document visible text, signs, logos, buildings, vegetation, roads, utilities, and nearby landmarks.
- Separate direct observations from interpretations.
- Record uncertainty where text or objects are unclear.

## 4. Generate Hypotheses
- Use distinctive clues to identify possible countries, regions, businesses, or landmarks.
- Avoid accepting the first plausible match.
- Keep alternative explanations open until evidence supports one.

## 5. Corroborate Independently
- Compare candidate locations with official business pages, maps, street-level imagery, and other reliable sources.
- Look for multiple matching features, such as storefront design, entrances, windows, road layout, and nearby structures.
- Record each source and explain what it establishes.

## 6. Assess Confidence
- High confidence requires several distinctive, independently corroborated matches.
- Moderate confidence means several clues agree but important uncertainty remains.
- Low confidence means the conclusion relies on limited or ambiguous clues.

## 7. Document Limitations
The target image's original source reportedly exposes location information. This case is therefore a practice investigation, not a fully blind geolocation exercise. The final report must distinguish independent observations from information obtained through source research.

## 8. Reproducibility
Record commands, source URLs, access dates, findings, and unresolved questions so another analyst can repeat the process.
